# OTPPhone Demand Validation: Target List and Outreach Dossier

**Prepared:** 5 June 2026
**Purpose:** The desk-researchable half of the demand-validation plan. It populates the plan's "named target list per segment" with real organisations, live accelerator calls, frameworks, events and entry routes, so whoever runs the interviews starts with a working list rather than a blank page. The interviews themselves remain a human task.
**How to read it:** Each segment has a short framing note and a table of named targets. Dates were correct at the time of writing and should be reconfirmed before you act, because calls and deadlines move.

> Etiquette note that applies throughout: you are approaching busy people, some of whom support users at real risk. Lead with listening, not a pitch. Do not tie up the Access Now emergency helpline with vendor questions, do not spend a warm defence introduction on an unprepared call, and do not describe OTPPhone as finished when it is not.

## Segment A: Journalists and NGOs (run first)

The fastest and most honest signal, reachable year-round through known organisations. Treat the trainers and information-security leads inside these bodies as the highest-value interviewees, because they shape what hundreds of journalists adopt. There is almost no money in this segment; you are validating problem, wedge and adoption, not price.

## What this dossier does not give you

- **Named individuals.** It lists organisations, programmes and public roles, not personal contacts. Your warm introducers (Cameron, the Plymouth cluster, a RUSI member) are how you turn an organisation into a person.
- **Guaranteed-current call status.** The DIANA deadline, the DASA reopening and the G-Cloud timetable were accurate at the time of writing. Reconfirm each on the official page before you rely on it.
- **A substitute for the interviews.** This is the prospect list. The validation only exists once the conversations in the plan actually happen and you have recorded what people do today and what they will commit to.

## Suggested first moves

1. Decide this month whether to apply to NATO DIANA before 3 July 2026; even a no is worth making deliberately.
2. Join the DASA mailing list so you catch the Open Call reopening.
3. Make one warm approach into the Plymouth cluster, both for end-user access and to find the commercial or student collaborator the plan needs.
4. Open the journalist segment in parallel, starting with one or two digital-security trainers rather than the large organisations' front doors.
